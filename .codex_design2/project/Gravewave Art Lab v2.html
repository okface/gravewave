01

The Language

A combat scene rendered at full fidelity. Everything from this point on is a study of how the parts work in isolation.

Five principles

i.
Ink firstEvery form is a black silhouette before it's anything else. Colour is applied like risograph layers — flat, separated, slightly mis-registered.
ii.
Hatched massVolume comes from cross-hatching, not gradients. The hatch direction tells you what surface you're on.
iii.
Colour as stateRed = damage / fire / threat. Blue = frost / chill. Gold = electricity / charge. Green = poison. Default is monochrome — colour earns its place.
iv.
Motion is decisiveEverything moves with intent. Particles travel, trail, fade. No static dressed-up frames.
v.
Top-down, alwaysThe camera is overhead. Spells lay on the ground, not in front of it.

Fireball

Dense vermilion comet with a real-time ember trail and smoke puffs that decay behind it. A black ink core keeps the silhouette legible against the paper.
Lightning Bolt

A jagged ink seam that flashes twice — first hit, then arc to the next target. Forks branch and decay. Strikes are punctual: present then gone.

Concentric ink rings draining inward, with motes pulled along a logarithmic spiral toward a black singularity. The most diegetic of the spells.

Ink cloud silhouette overhead, periodic forking strikes that flash and snap. The ground inside the area gets faint scorch marks at each tick.

07

How this lands in the codebase

Per-method swap Every demo here calls into the same shape of Renderer.draw* the game uses today. drawPlayer(ctx,p,t), drawEnemyShape(ctx,e), drawProjectiles, drawAreas, drawParticles, drawHUD — drop-in replacements.
Particles are real Each spell maintains a small particle pool driven by game.runElapsed with per-particle x,y,vx,vy,a,life. The trails you see are simulations, not painted decals — same approach scales to thousands.
Top-down respected The wall of flame and gravity well lay flat on the ground. No profile-rendered FX. Camera direction is consistent across the whole pass.
Colour budget Default state monochrome ink-on-vellum. Five accent colours (vermilion, indigo, ochre, green, gold) carry meaning — they only appear for state, damage, or magic. This makes a hatched paper world that pops when the spells fire.
Status legibility Runes rotate at distinct rates so a triple-stacked enemy is parseable at a glance. No relying on opacity to disambiguate.
Next steps Lift these draw functions into Renderer; tune colour values against in-engine framerate; add an onHit particle burst (sparks + ink splatter) per damage event so kills feel weighty.
