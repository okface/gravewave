01

Three Directions

Each direction is a coherent system: a palette, a mark-making language (how shapes are drawn), a light model, and a motion grammar. They share the existing layout — phone-shaped frame, vertical lanes, AFK player center — but differ in what the world feels like.

Ash & Ember

PAINTERLY · WARM · CINEMATIC

Soft volumetric light, ember motes drifting up, painterly silhouettes. Reads as a haunted vigil — graves smouldering in a dusk fog. Emphasises atmosphere over readability when not in combat; spells punch through with high-contrast bursts.

Ink & Vellum

WOODCUT · HIGH-CONTRAST · GRAPHIC

Black ink on aged paper. Hatching, hard edges, almost no anti-aliasing. Spells are bold flat shapes that overprint like risograph layers. Status effects read instantly because everything else is monochrome — colour is reserved for state.

Neon Reliquary

ARCADE · NEON · RHYTHMIC

Pure dark, then ribbons of saturated light. Enemies are wireframes that crack apart. Spells are pulses on a beat. Closer to a vector arcade game than a fantasy roguelite — leans into the "auto-combat is a light show" angle.

08

How this lands in the codebase

Every demo here calls the same shape of function the game already exposes. The Renderer is pure (per ARCHITECTURE.md), so swapping it is non-destructive.

Per-method swap Each draw method on Renderer (drawPlayer, drawEnemyShape, drawProjectiles, etc.) is independent. You can adopt one direction's enemies, another's spells. Mix freely.
No data changes Enemy shapes are dispatched on e.def.shape; spell colours come from p.color. None of the registries need editing — the look lives entirely in the renderer.
Animations are time-driven All FX here use game.runElapsed (or performance.now()) plus per-entity state (flash, iframes, statuses). No new entity fields required — same data, new appearance.
Status rings stay readable The current ring stack is preserved in every direction. Slow / ignite / shock have distinct hues and dash patterns so a triple-stacked enemy is still parseable at a glance.
Particles & trails Each direction proposes a different particle vocabulary (ember, hatch-mark, voxel) — implementable in drawParticles without touching the spawn logic.
Pick & mix path forward I'd suggest: choose one direction as the floor, then pull standout pieces from the others (e.g. Ink's status runes, Neon's chain lightning). The lab will let you A/B them.
